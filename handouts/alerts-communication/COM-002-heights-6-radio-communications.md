<p class="lede">Set up the BTECH UV-PRO for <strong>Heights 6</strong>, then use short, disciplined calls on or off a directed emergency net.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Life safety comes first.</strong> Call 911 by telephone when available for an immediate threat to life or property. Do not delay evacuation, enter a hazard, or rely on the radio as your only way to summon help.</div>

## Program Heights 6

<div class="local-info">
<p><strong>Channel name:</strong> Heights 6 &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>Radio:</strong> BTECH UV-PRO</p>
<p><strong>Receive (RX):</strong> 462.38750 MHz &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>RX DCS:</strong> 331N</p>
<p><strong>Transmit (TX):</strong> 467.38750 MHz &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>TX DCS:</strong> 331N</p>
<p><strong>Repeater site:</strong> KOCE-TV transmitter site, 16711 E Skyline Dr, La Habra Heights, CA 90631</p>
</div>

<div class="grid">
<div>
<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Connect.</strong> Open the UV-PRO app on the paired phone, connect to the correct radio, and read its current channel list.</li>
<li><strong>Create the channel.</strong> Select an unused memory, name it <strong>Heights 6</strong>, and choose separate RX and TX fields.</li>
<li><strong>Enter the values exactly:</strong> RX 462.38750 MHz and TX 467.38750 MHz. Do not reverse them.</li>
<li><strong>Set both digital squelch fields</strong> to DCS/DQT <strong>331</strong> with <strong>normal (N)</strong> polarity. Do not select inverted/reverse polarity.</li>
<li><strong>Write/synchronize</strong> the channel to the radio, reconnect, and confirm the name and all four values. Leave bandwidth, power, and other fields at Firewatch-approved values.</li>
</ol>

<div class="warning warning--caution">App labels can change by version. If a screen does not clearly show separate RX/TX frequencies and separate RX/TX DCS values, stop and ask the Firewatch radio lead to program it. Never “test” by transmitting on an unknown channel.</div>
</div>

<div>
## Check the radio before duty

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Battery charged; spare or cable ready</li>
<li>Antenna secure; volume audible; <strong>Heights 6</strong> selected</li>
<li>Assigned three-digit callsign known (for example, <strong>145</strong>)</li>
<li>Push-to-talk protected from accidental presses</li>
<li>Receive checked; transmit checked only when authorized</li>
</ul></div>

## Before a net begins

<p>Monitor first and keep Heights 6 clear of casual incident traffic. To reach a member, say their callsign, then yours: <strong>“145, 238.”</strong> Wait for “238, go ahead.” Use plain language and confirmed facts.</p>

<p><strong>Identification:</strong> members use their assigned three-digit callsign. Address city personnel by title: <strong>City Manager</strong>, <strong>Public Works</strong>, <strong>Fire Captain</strong>, <strong>Fire Marshal</strong>, or <strong>Fire Chief</strong>. Follow instructions for any additional legally required station ID.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="sources">Local frequencies, site, naming, callsigns, titles, and net cadence: La Habra Heights Fire Watch operational brief received September 24, 2026. Programming workflow requires verification against the current BTECH UV-PRO manual and app before approval.</div>

<!-- pagebreak -->

<p class="lede">On a directed net, Net Control manages the channel. Listen, wait to be recognized, and report the essential facts.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Emergency traffic has priority.</strong> If there is an immediate danger, wait for a break, say <strong>“Emergency traffic”</strong> and your callsign, then release the push-to-talk button. Net Control will answer. Do not interrupt routine traffic.</div>

## Directed-net procedure

<div class="grid">
<div>
<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Listen before transmitting.</strong> Do not answer a call meant for someone else and do not transmit during Net Control's situation report.</li>
<li><strong>Call:</strong> “Net Control, [your callsign].” Release and wait. If unanswered, pause and try once more.</li>
<li><strong>Wait to be recognized.</strong> When Net Control says “[callsign], go ahead,” press, pause briefly, and give one concise report.</li>
<li><strong>Report:</strong> location; direct observation and time; threats; action taken; and help needed. Separate observation from rumor.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm instructions.</strong> Read back critical locations, numbers, and assignments. End with your callsign, then return to monitoring.</li>
</ol>
</div>

<div>
<h2>Net Control updates</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>High activity:</strong> every 15 minutes—:00, :15, :30, :45</li>
<li><strong>Quieter:</strong> every 30 minutes—:00, :30</li>
<li><strong>Stand-down:</strong> every 60 minutes—:00</li>
</ul>
<p>Hold routine calls when an update is due. Urgent or emergency traffic does not wait.</p>

<h2>Good radio habits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Think first; keep the microphone close; press, pause, speak clearly.</li>
<li>Use street names, addresses, landmarks, and directions—not “over here.”</li>
<li>Say “affirmative” or “negative” when clarity matters.</li>
<li>Do not share sensitive details, speculate, argue, or acknowledge every broadcast.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

## Sample exchanges

<div class="grid">
<div>
<p><strong>Routine report</strong><br>
<strong>145:</strong> “Net Control, 145.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “145, go ahead.”<br>
<strong>145:</strong> “Skyline at Cypress: light gray smoke north of the intersection, first seen two minutes ago. No flame visible. 145.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “145, observe from a safe location and report changes.”<br>
<strong>145:</strong> “Observe safely and report changes. 145.”</p>
</div>

<div>
<p><strong>Emergency traffic</strong><br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Emergency traffic, 238.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “All stations hold. 238, go ahead with emergency traffic.”<br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Arcing power line across East Road at 17000. Road blocked; people moving back. Request response. 238.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “238, keep everyone clear; response requested. Report fire.”<br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Keep clear and report fire. 238.”</p>
</div>
</div>

## Closing or leaving the net

<p><strong>Do not leave silently.</strong> Ask to check out or report a radio/battery change. At closure, note final instructions and monitor if directed. After duty, report problems, recharge, and do not alter Heights 6.</p>
