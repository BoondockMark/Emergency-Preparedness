<p class="lede">Set up the BTECH UV-PRO for <strong>Heights 6</strong>, then use short, disciplined calls on or off a directed emergency net.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Life safety comes first.</strong> Call 911 by telephone when available for an immediate threat to life or property. Do not delay evacuation, enter a hazard, or rely on the radio as your only way to summon help.</div>

## Program Heights 6

<div class="local-info">
<p><strong>Channel name:</strong> Heights 6 &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>Radio:</strong> BTECH UV-PRO</p>
<p><strong>Receive (RX):</strong> 462.38750 MHz &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>RX DCS:</strong> 331N</p>
<p><strong>Transmit (TX):</strong> 467.38750 MHz &nbsp; | &nbsp; <strong>TX DCS:</strong> 331N</p>
<p><strong>Repeater site:</strong> KOCE-TV transmitter site, 16711 E Skyline Dr, La Habra Heights, CA 90631</p>
</div>

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Open the UV-PRO programming app</strong> on the paired phone, connect to the correct radio, and read/synchronize the radio's current channel list before editing it.</li>
<li><strong>Select an unused memory channel.</strong> Enter the channel name <strong>Heights 6</strong>. Choose separate RX and TX frequencies (sometimes shown as repeater or duplex programming).</li>
<li><strong>Enter the values exactly:</strong> RX 462.38750 and TX 467.38750 MHz. Do not reverse them.</li>
<li><strong>Set both digital squelch fields</strong> to DCS/DQT <strong>331</strong> with <strong>normal (N)</strong> polarity. Do not select inverted/reverse polarity.</li>
<li><strong>Leave every other field</strong>—including bandwidth, power, and busy-channel lockout—at the Firewatch-approved template value. Write/synchronize the change to the radio and confirm the channel still reads <strong>Heights 6</strong> after reconnecting.</li>
</ol>

<div class="warning warning--caution">App labels can change by version. If a screen does not clearly show separate RX/TX frequencies and separate RX/TX DCS values, stop and ask the Firewatch radio lead to program it. Never “test” by transmitting on an unknown channel.</div>

## Check the radio before duty

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Battery charged; spare or charging cable ready</li>
<li>Antenna secure; volume audible; <strong>Heights 6</strong> selected</li>
<li>Your assigned three-digit callsign known (for example, <strong>145</strong>)</li>
<li>Radio carried where the push-to-talk button will not be pressed accidentally</li>
<li>Receive check completed; transmit check made only when authorized</li>
</ul></div>

## Before a net begins

<p>Monitor first. Keep Heights 6 clear of casual traffic during an incident. To reach another member, say their callsign first, then yours: <strong>“145, 238.”</strong> Wait for “238, go ahead,” then speak. Use plain language—not ten-codes—and give only confirmed facts.</p>

<p><strong>City personnel:</strong> address them by title, such as <strong>City Manager</strong>, <strong>Public Works</strong>, <strong>Fire Captain</strong>, <strong>Fire Marshal</strong>, or <strong>Fire Chief</strong>. Members use their assigned three-digit callsign. Follow the radio lead's instructions for any additional legally required station identification.</p>

<div class="sources">Local frequencies, site, naming, callsigns, titles, and net cadence: La Habra Heights Fire Watch operational brief received September 24, 2026. Programming workflow requires verification against the current BTECH UV-PRO manual and app before approval.</div>

<!-- pagebreak -->

<p class="lede">On a directed net, Net Control manages the channel. Listen, wait to be recognized, and report the essential facts.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Emergency traffic has priority.</strong> If there is an immediate danger, wait for a break, say <strong>“Emergency traffic”</strong> and your callsign, then release the push-to-talk button. Net Control will answer. Do not interrupt routine traffic.</div>

## Directed-net procedure

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Listen before transmitting.</strong> Do not answer a call meant for someone else and do not transmit during Net Control's situation report.</li>
<li><strong>Call Net Control:</strong> “Net Control, [your callsign].” Release the button and wait. If no answer, pause and try once more; do not make repeated calls over other traffic.</li>
<li><strong>Wait to be recognized.</strong> When Net Control says “[callsign], go ahead,” press, pause briefly, and give one concise report.</li>
<li><strong>Report in order:</strong> exact location; what you directly observe; when observed; people or structures threatened; action already taken; and what help or instruction you need. Separate observation from rumor.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm instructions.</strong> Read back critical locations, numbers, and assignments. End with your callsign, then return to monitoring.</li>
</ol>

<div class="grid">
<div>
<h2>Net Control updates</h2>
<p>Net Control reports known information on a predictable schedule:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>High activity:</strong> every 15 minutes at :00, :15, :30, and :45</li>
<li><strong>Quieter operations:</strong> every 30 minutes at :00 and :30</li>
<li><strong>Stand-down phase:</strong> every 60 minutes at :00</li>
</ul>
<p>Hold routine calls when an update is due. Urgent or emergency traffic does not wait for the schedule.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h2>Good radio habits</h2>
<ul>
<li>Think first; keep the microphone close; press, pause, speak clearly.</li>
<li>Use street names, addresses, landmarks, and directions—not “over here.”</li>
<li>Say “affirmative” or “negative” instead of yes/no when clarity matters.</li>
<li>Never transmit names or sensitive personal details unless Net Control requests them.</li>
<li>Do not speculate, argue, or acknowledge every broadcast unless asked.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

## Sample exchanges

<p><strong>Routine report</strong><br>
<strong>145:</strong> “Net Control, 145.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “145, go ahead.”<br>
<strong>145:</strong> “At Skyline Drive and Cypress Street, light gray smoke is visible north of the intersection, first observed two minutes ago. No flames visible. Request confirmation of assignment. 145.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “145, remain at a safe observation point and report any change.”<br>
<strong>145:</strong> “Remain at a safe observation point and report changes. 145.”</p>

<p><strong>Emergency traffic</strong><br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Emergency traffic, 238.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “All stations hold. 238, go ahead with emergency traffic.”<br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Downed power line across East Road at number 17000. Line is arcing; road is blocked. I am moving people back. Request Public Works and fire response. 238.”<br>
<strong>Net Control:</strong> “238, keep everyone clear; response is being requested. Report any fire.”<br>
<strong>238:</strong> “Keep everyone clear and report any fire. 238.”</p>

## Closing or leaving the net

<p><strong>Do not leave silently.</strong> Ask Net Control to check out or report a radio/battery change. When Net Control closes the net, note any final instructions and keep monitoring if directed. After duty, report radio or coverage problems, recharge equipment, and do not erase Heights 6 or change approved settings.</p>
