<p class="lede">Use more than one way to receive warnings. Start with the City’s official signup page, then plan for missed messages and loss of connectivity.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Act without delay.</strong> Follow current official protective instructions. Do not wait for a second alert or neighborhood confirmation when danger is immediate or officials direct evacuation.</div>

## Know the difference

<div class="grid">
<div>
<h3>Official emergency instructions</h3>
<p>Messages issued by an authorized government public-safety agency may direct you to evacuate, shelter in place, avoid an area, or take another protective action. Follow the message and later updates from the issuing authority.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h3>Neighborhood coordination</h3>
<p>Neighbors may relay an official message, check on one another, or share observed conditions. They do <strong>not</strong> issue evacuation orders or replace 911, public-safety dispatch, or an authorized alerting agency.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Set up layered alerts

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Enable Wireless Emergency Alerts.</strong> Check the emergency-alert settings on each compatible mobile phone. WEA alerts do not require enrollment; device, carrier, location, and settings affect receipt.</li>
<li><strong>Enroll in the verified local system.</strong> For La Habra Heights, start at <a href="https://www.lhhcity.org/478/Be-Informed">lhhcity.org/478/Be-Informed</a> and use the City-linked signup form. The City describes Everbridge notifications; the embedded form is branded Nixle. Record your confirmation below. Add home, work, school, and caregiving locations when the official system permits.</li>
<li><strong>Add a non-cellular backup.</strong> Choose a verified broadcast source, weather radio, landline notification, or a person who can relay official information.</li>
<li><strong>Make alerts accessible.</strong> Set preferred languages and sound, vibration, caption, screen-reader, or other access features where available.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm before sharing.</strong> When safe, check the issuing agency and time. Do not delay a protective action to verify a message. Use its official website or verified account; do not treat a screenshot or forwarded post as a new instruction.</li>
</ol>
