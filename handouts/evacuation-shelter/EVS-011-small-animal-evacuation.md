<p class="lede">Prepare to leave safely with every household pet and care for them during a three-day displacement. This guide is for La Habra Heights Fire Watch members and residents with dogs, cats, birds, and other non-livestock pets.</p>

<div class="warning warning--danger"><strong>Leave immediate danger.</strong> Take pets you can safely reach, but never delay escape to search for or capture an animal. Do not enter smoke, floodwater, damaged buildings, contaminated areas, or closed evacuation zones. From safety, tell emergency officials about animals left behind. Call 911 for immediate threats to human life.</div>

## The plan at a glance

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Prepare now.</strong> Assign handlers and backups, practice loading, identify destinations, and pack supplies for each pet.</li>
<li><strong>Act early.</strong> When authorities advise preparation, bring pets into a safe, accessible area if it is safe to do so. Ready carriers and transport before conditions worsen.</li>
<li><strong>Leave as directed.</strong> Use current official routes and instructions. Load pets securely and take their supplies and records.</li>
</ol>

## Match your action to the hazard

| Emergency | What to do |
|---|---|
| Fire | For a building fire, get out immediately. For a nearby wildfire, prepare and move pets early when safe; follow evacuation instructions. Never return to a burning building for a pet. |
| Earthquake | During shaking, Drop, Cover, and Hold On; do not chase pets or run outside. After shaking stops, secure pets when safe and follow official instructions about evacuation and damaged areas. |
| Flooding | Move early along safe routes before access is cut off. Never walk or drive through floodwater to reach or transport an animal. |
| Hazardous materials | Follow officials' direction to evacuate or shelter in place. If told to shelter, keep pets with you in the designated indoor area and follow ventilation instructions. Report suspected contamination before entering transport or a center. |

<p><strong>Fire Watch:</strong> help residents prepare, share official instructions, and relay assistance requests. Do not enter restricted areas or attempt unsafe rescues.</p>

<!-- page-title: Plan Before an Emergency -->

<p class="lede">Complete this checklist with everyone who may care for your pets. Keep the private household plan with your go kit; post only the window notice.</p>

## Planning checklist

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li><strong>List every pet.</strong> Record species, name, appearance, usual location, hiding places, identification, and handling or medical needs. Keep recent photos, including one with the owner.</li>
<li><strong>Assign a handler and backup.</strong> Make sure each accepts the role. Arrange written permission and safe access for a trusted caregiver if you are away. Keep keys and access codes off the public notice.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare suitable equipment.</strong> Have a secure carrier, kennel, cage, or species-appropriate restraint for each pet. Label equipment with the animal's name and your phone number.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm transport capacity.</strong> Fit every pet, person, and essential supply in the planned vehicle safely. Arrange a backup driver and vehicle; do not rely on a later return trip.</li>
<li><strong>Choose destinations and two routes.</strong> Include a backup outside the affected area. Check accepted species, required equipment, and care arrangements. Review the City's <a href="https://www.lhhcity.org/478/Be-Informed">Be Informed</a> page for official alert information.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare health records.</strong> Keep vaccination and medication records, microchip details, ownership information, veterinary contacts, and care instructions in a waterproof pouch, with an offline digital copy.</li>
<li><strong>Practice.</strong> Rehearse calm carrier use and loading. Time the process and allow extra time in your early departure plan. Update after changes and check supplies regularly.</li>
</ul></div>

<div class="local-info"><strong>La Habra Heights arrangements — [VERIFY] before distribution:</strong> The planning arrangement is for city-provided evacuation centers to accept small animals on leash or in kennels or other suitable enclosures. Confirm accepted species, intake requirements, and the animal-assistance process with the City. During an incident, use the location officials announce; ask about unusual pets before arrival when possible.</div>

<!-- page-title: Pack a Three Day Pet Go Kit -->

<p class="lede">Pack for each pet's full food, water, medication, and care needs for 72 hours away from home. Store supplies where they can leave with you.</p>

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li><strong>Food:</strong> three days of the usual diet, labeled portions and feeding instructions, bowls, and a manual can opener if needed. Include special diets and species-specific feed.</li>
<li><strong>Water:</strong> three days of safe drinking water for every pet, plus water needed for food preparation or care. Ask your veterinarian about amounts for your animals; needs vary with species, size, health, and temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Medication:</strong> at least three days of prescribed medicines and administration supplies, labeled directions, prescription details, and a refill contact. Ask the veterinarian about refrigeration and power outages before an emergency.</li>
<li><strong>Containment:</strong> labeled carriers or kennels, secure latches, leash and harness where appropriate, and a backup restraint. Pets need space and ventilation suitable for the species.</li>
<li><strong>Identification and records:</strong> current photos, medical and vaccination records, microchip and owner details, emergency contacts, and written care instructions.</li>
<li><strong>Sanitation:</strong> waste bags, absorbent liners, litter and a portable pan where needed, bedding, paper towels, gloves, and cleaning supplies safe for the species.</li>
<li><strong>Comfort and handling:</strong> familiar bedding, a towel, and a suitable comfort item. Include species-specific habitat and handling supplies listed on the next page.</li>
<li><strong>Health and power:</strong> a pet first-aid kit and instructions, chargers or batteries for essential equipment, and a safe backup plan for powered habitat or medical equipment.</li>
</ul></div>

## Plan if displacement lasts longer

<p>Three days is this handout's planning period, not a limit on how long evacuation may last. CDC recommends a two-week supply of pet food, water, and medications in its disaster kit guidance. Keep additional reserves when feasible and plan how to obtain refills, specialty feed, and habitat supplies if return is delayed.</p>

<p><strong>Medication safety:</strong> give only medicines prescribed for that animal, following veterinary directions.</p>

<!-- page-title: Prepare for Each Kind of Pet -->

<p class="lede">A leash or standard dog kennel will not meet every pet's needs. Plan transport, temporary housing, and power needs for the actual species you keep.</p>

| Pet | Transport and temporary care |
|---|---|
| Dogs | Use a secure leash and well-fitted harness or collar; prepare a suitable kennel for transport and center requirements. Keep identification attached. Record bite risk, fear triggers, and handling needs. |
| Cats | Prepare a secure, ventilated carrier for each cat. Practice carrier use before an emergency. Pack litter supplies; open carriers only in a secure enclosed space approved by the receiving site. |
| Birds | Use a secure travel cage or carrier. Remove unsecured objects that could injure the bird during travel. Pack familiar feed, water equipment, and cage liners. Protect ventilation and avoid smoke, fumes, and temperature extremes. |
| Rabbits, guinea pigs, rodents, ferrets, and other small mammals | Use an escape-resistant carrier suited to the animal. Pack the normal diet, hay where required, bedding, and familiar feeding and watering equipment. Separate incompatible animals. |
| Reptiles and amphibians | Arrange secure species-specific transport and housing. Record required temperature, humidity, lighting, diet, and safe handling. Plan temperature monitoring and a safe source of heat or power; prevent direct contact with heat sources. |
| Fish and aquatic pets | Develop a species-specific transport plan with an aquatic veterinarian or knowledgeable aquarium specialist. Prepare suitable containers and a plan for water quality, oxygen, temperature, and backup power. Do not assume a full aquarium can be moved safely. |
| Other non-livestock pets | Identify containment, handling, diet, environmental needs, and a suitable receiving location in advance. Confirm acceptance of unusual or potentially dangerous species directly with the site. |

<p><strong>Service animals:</strong> keep the animal with its handler whenever possible. Tell transport and center staff that it is a service animal; service-animal access differs from pet intake. See STH-006 for planning with the handler.</p>

<div class="sources">Species guidance: AVMA, <a href="https://ebusiness.avma.org/files/productdownloads/STWF_English.pdf">Save the Whole Family</a>; ASPCA, <a href="https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/disaster-preparedness">Disaster Preparedness</a>. Accessed October 29, 2026. Arrange individual veterinary instructions for specialized care.</div>

<!-- page-title: Evacuate and Care for Pets Away from Home -->

## Evacuation checklist

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Follow official protective instructions. If safe, confirm the announced center and route; use your alternate if directed. Do not delay escape to make calls.</li>
<li>Secure pets in appropriate carriers or restraints before opening exterior doors. Keep incompatible animals separate. Take your pet roster, go kit, records, and medications.</li>
<li>Secure carriers in the passenger area according to equipment instructions. Maintain ventilation and safe temperatures. Never put pets in a closed trunk or leave them unattended in a parked vehicle.</li>
<li>Count and identify every pet as it leaves. If safe and time permits, update the window notice with remaining, evacuated, and unaccounted-for animals and the time.</li>
<li>Leave by the safe route officials direct. Keep carriers closed during travel. Report animals left behind from a safe location; never re-enter a restricted area.</li>
</ul></div>

## At the evacuation center

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Check in and recount pets. Tell staff about species, handling risks, medical needs, and any suspected smoke, floodwater, or chemical exposure before intake. Ask for veterinary help when needed.</li>
<li>Follow leash, kennel, separation, sanitation, and feeding rules. Confirm whether pets stay with owners or in a separate area and who provides daily care.</li>
<li>If care is transferred, record each animal's identification, receiving person or organization, location, phone, time, and reunification instructions. Obtain an intake record.</li>
<li>Give food, water, and prescribed medicines on schedule; record doses. Provide a quiet area when available. Wash hands after handling animals, equipment, or waste.</li>
</ul></div>

<p><strong>Seek urgent veterinary help</strong> for trouble breathing, collapse, burns, major injury, seizures, or suspected poisoning. Notify center staff immediately. Do not improvise treatment or decontamination with household chemicals.</p>

## If a pet is missing or still at the property

<p>Report the address, species, number, description, last known location and time, and handling risks to the incident's animal-assistance contact. Share photos and microchip details. Check designated animal shelters and lost-pet systems. A window notice provides information; it does not submit a rescue request or guarantee rescue.</p>

<p>La Habra Heights contracts with Los Angeles County Animal Care and Control. Use the City's <a href="https://lhhcity.org/394/Animal-Control">Animal Control page</a> for service and lost-pet information. Incident-specific intake locations may differ.</p>

## Return safely

<p>Return only when officials allow it. Before releasing pets indoors or into a yard, check for damaged fencing, broken glass, ash, chemicals, standing water, and other hazards. Keep pets restrained while checking the area. Monitor their health and behavior, replace supplies, and update the plan and window notice. Use EVS-009 for more return guidance.</p>
