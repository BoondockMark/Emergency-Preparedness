<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Fill sandbags efficiently using a multi-bag filling fixture.</p>
<h2>Before you begin</h2>
<p>Sandbags can redirect shallow stormwater runoff from vulnerable areas of your property. They work best when placed before heavy rain arrives.</p>
<h2>What you’ll need</h2>
<div class="grid sandbag-equipment">
<ul>
<li>Empty woven sandbags</li>
<li>Clean sand or suitable granular fill</li>
<li>Multi-bag filling fixture</li>
<li>Shovel</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li>Gloves and sturdy footwear</li>
<li>Eye protection when sand is dusty</li>
<li>A clear, level work area</li>
<li>A partner for lifting</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="warning warning--caution">A filled bag may weigh 35–40 lb. Use a partner or lighter bags if needed. Work on level, dry ground; keep hands and feet clear when lifting the fixture. Leave bags one-half to two-thirds full, with fabric to fold underneath; remove excess sand.</div>

<!-- page-title: Planning a Sandbag Barrier -->
<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Understand where water comes from and where it can safely go.</p>
<h2>First, observe the water’s path</h2>
<ol class="procedure" start="1" style="counter-reset: step 0">
<li><strong>Where is the water coming from?</strong> Look for runoff from hillsides, driveways, roofs, and low areas.</li>
<li><strong>What needs protection?</strong> Identify vulnerable doors, garages, low windows, and openings.</li>
<li><strong>Where can the water safely go?</strong> Find an existing drainage path on your property.</li>
</ol>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-plan sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="plan-barrier-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/plan-barrier.svg" alt="Plan view of a house: uphill runoff meets a low sandbag line and follows arrows around the doorway and driveway toward an existing safe drainage path.">
<figcaption id="plan-barrier-caption">Plan a short diversion to an existing safe drainage path.</figcaption>
</figure>
<h2>Select the barrier location</h2>
<div class="grid ">
<ul>
<li>Place bags on firm, level ground.</li>
<li>Intercept runoff before it reaches the opening.</li>
<li>Use a short line that redirects water.</li>
<li>Keep drains, exits, and access routes open.</li>
<li>Do not redirect runoff toward a neighbor, road, or another doorway.</li>
</ul>
<div class="warning warning--danger">Leave if water is moving fast, rising around you, or cutting off your exit. Do not enter flowing water or build beside an unstable slope or wall.</div>
</div>
</div>

<!-- page-title: Building a Sandbag Barrier -->
<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Overlap bags, stagger joints, and maintain a stable base.</p>
<h2>Prepare the ground</h2>
<ol class="procedure" start="1" style="counter-reset: step 0">
<li><strong>Clear the area.</strong> Remove loose debris and fill low spots.</li>
<li><strong>Establish the line.</strong> Identify the water-facing side and intended direction of flow.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Place the first course</h2>
<ol class="procedure" start="3" style="counter-reset: step 2">
<li><strong>Lay bags flat.</strong> Fold the empty end underneath and toward incoming water.</li>
<li><strong>Overlap adjoining bags.</strong> Lay the filled part over the previous bag’s empty end.</li>
<li><strong>Seat the bags.</strong> Press or tamp firmly to reduce gaps.</li>
</ol>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-overlap sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="overlap-bags-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/overlap-bags.svg" alt="Four flat bags overlap in a line, with each filled portion covering the previous bag’s folded empty end and water flowing along the row.">
<figcaption id="overlap-bags-caption">Top view — overlap adjoining bags.</figcaption>
</figure>
<h2>Add additional courses</h2>
<ol class="procedure" start="6" style="counter-reset: step 5">
<li><strong>Stagger every course.</strong> Offset joints by about half a bag.</li>
<li><strong>Maintain a wide base.</strong> Keep the barrier low with a stable, stepped profile.</li>
<li><strong>Check the ends.</strong> Connect to suitable higher ground or provide a safe outlet.</li>
</ol>
<div class="grid ">
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-stacks" aria-labelledby="stagger-courses-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/stagger-courses.svg" alt="Three courses of bags have offset joints; the joints of the middle course cover the centers of the bags below.">
<figcaption id="stagger-courses-caption">Stagger each course by about half a bag.</figcaption>
</figure>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-stacks sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="wide-base-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/wide-base.svg" alt="Cross-section of a low barrier with three bags at the base, two above, and one at the top; labels identify the water side and protected side.">
<figcaption id="wide-base-caption">Keep a wide, stepped base.</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<h2>Inspect the finished barrier</h2>
<ul>
<li>Check for gaps, uncovered joints, and displaced bags.</li>
<li>Look for water flowing around the ends or pooling behind the line.</li>
<li>Sandbags are temporary and do not form a watertight seal.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<!-- page-title: Protecting Vulnerable Openings -->
<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Choose the barrier arrangement that fits the runoff problem.</p>
<h2>A. Exterior doorways</h2>
<div class="grid ">
<ul>
<li>Place a short, low barrier before water reaches the threshold.</li>
<li>Extend it beyond both sides of the opening.</li>
<li>Avoid blocking the only safe exit.</li>
<li>Check for water entering around the ends or through other openings.</li>
<li>Do not rely on sandbags to make a doorway watertight.</li>
</ul>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--contain sandbag-opening" aria-labelledby="protect-doorway-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/bdf6c66b-52ef-4ce1-ab30-8bb75678146c.png" alt="A low barrier of overlapping sandbags extends across a doorway and beyond both sides.">
<figcaption id="protect-doorway-caption">Doorway protection. Keep another safe exit available.</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<h2>B. Garage doors and sloping driveways</h2>
<div class="grid ">
<ul>
<li>Intercept runoff uphill of the garage door.</li>
<li>Use an angled or curved line to redirect water.</li>
<li>Lead water to an existing suitable drainage path.</li>
<li>Avoid directing water toward a neighbor, road, or other doorway.</li>
<li>Keep the evacuation route open.</li>
</ul>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-opening sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="protect-garage-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/protect-garage.svg" alt="A low angled line lies across part of a sloping driveway in front of a garage; an arrow follows the line toward a safe drainage path.">
<figcaption id="protect-garage-caption">Driveway diversion toward safe drainage.</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<h2>C. Low windows and utility openings</h2>
<div class="grid ">
<ul>
<li>Identify low windows, window wells, and utility penetrations.</li>
<li>Use a low diversion barrier to reduce surface runoff.</li>
<li>Keep required ventilation, window escape routes, and utility access clear.</li>
</ul>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-opening" aria-labelledby="protect-low-window-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/protect-low-window.svg" alt="A low sandbag diversion stands in front of a window well while the window and required access remain clear.">
<figcaption id="protect-low-window-caption">Low window well protection.</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
</div>

<!-- page-title: Understanding Water Movement -->
<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Recognize bypass, seepage, and unintended pooling.</p>
<h2>1. Water flowing around the ends</h2>
<p>A line that stops below higher ground may send water around its end.</p>
<div class="grid sandbag-water-row">
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-water sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="water-around-ends-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/water-around-ends.svg" alt="Runoff follows a curved arrow around the free end of a sandbag line instead of reaching a safe outlet.">
<figcaption id="water-around-ends-caption">Water flowing around the ends.</figcaption>
</figure>
<div><h3>What to do</h3><ul>
<li>Extend or adjust the line only from safe, dry ground.</li>
<li>Use suitable higher ground or an existing safe outlet.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<h2>2. Water seeping through the barrier</h2>
<p>Water can pass between bags, through fabric, and underneath.</p>
<div class="grid sandbag-water-row">
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-water sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="water-seepage-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/water-seepage.svg" alt="Labeled arrows pass through joints and underneath the sandbag barrier, showing that bags are not watertight.">
<figcaption id="water-seepage-caption">Water seeping through the barrier.</figcaption>
</figure>
<div><h3>What to do</h3><ul>
<li>Seat bags firmly and cover joints.</li>
<li>Follow local guidance for water-side plastic sheeting.</li>
<li>Inspect for leakage from safe ground.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<h2>3. Water pooling behind the barrier</h2>
<p>A poorly placed barrier can trap water on the protected side.</p>
<div class="grid sandbag-water-row">
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--diagram figure--contain sandbag-water sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="water-pooling-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/water-pooling.svg" alt="A curved sandbag line traps a pool on the building side; eroded ground beneath the end indicates possible undercutting.">
<figcaption id="water-pooling-caption">Water pooling behind the barrier.</figcaption>
</figure>
<div><h3>What to do</h3><ul>
<li>Do not work in flowing water or on unstable ground.</li>
<li>Move to safety if erosion, displacement, or a rapid rise occurs.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="warning warning--danger">Leave if conditions become unsafe. Undercutting, displaced bags, or rising water can cause the barrier to fail.</div>
</div>

<!-- page-title: Sandbag Field Checklist -->
<div class="sandbag-page">
<p class="lede">Prepare early, inspect safely, and remove temporary barriers when conditions permit.</p>
<div class="grid sandbag-field-row">
<div><h2>Before the storm</h2>
<div class="checklist sandbag-checklist"><ul>
<li>Identify vulnerable openings and runoff paths.</li>
<li>Locate a safe, functioning drainage route.</li>
<li>Clear accessible drains and drainage channels.</li>
<li>Obtain sandbags, sand, and necessary tools.</li>
<li>Fill and stage bags on stable ground.</li>
<li>Build the diversion before water arrives.</li>
<li>Check that exits remain accessible.</li>
</ul>
</div><h2>During the storm</h2>
<div class="checklist sandbag-checklist"><ul>
<li>Observe runoff from a safe location.</li>
<li>Watch for water bypassing the barrier.</li>
<li>Check for unexpected pooling or seepage.</li>
<li>Stay away from moving water and unstable slopes.</li>
<li>Stop work and leave if conditions become dangerous.</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
<figure class="figure figure--full figure--contain sandbag-field sandbag-flow" aria-labelledby="field-check-scene-caption">
<img src="../assets/handouts/SKL-001/d7168f45-a11f-4ace-9851-2d6b47fd6ffe.png" alt="An overlapping sandbag line follows a runoff channel past a doorway.">
<figcaption id="field-check-scene-caption">Inspect from safe ground; keep exits open.</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<h2>After the storm</h2>
<div class="checklist sandbag-checklist"><ul>
<li>Wait until conditions are safe before approaching.</li>
<li>Inspect for erosion, unstable ground, and displaced bags.</li>
<li>Remove temporary barriers when no longer needed.</li>
<li>Do not dump used sand into drains or natural channels.</li>
<li>Follow local disposal guidance for wet or contaminated bags.</li>
<li>Correct drainage problems before the next storm.</li>
</ul>
</div><div class="warning warning--caution">Sandbags redirect and slow water; they do not make a home floodproof. Do not enter floodwater, approach unstable slopes, or stay in a threatened structure to maintain a barrier.</div>
