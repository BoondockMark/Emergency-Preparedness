<p class="lede">A quick reminder after recognized STOP THE BLEED® or equivalent instructor-led training—not a substitute for hands-on practice.</p>

<div class="warning warning--danger"><strong>Life-threatening bleeding is an emergency.</strong> Call 911 or your local emergency number now. Do not delay emergency activation to read this handout. Follow the dispatcher’s instructions.</div>

<div class="warning warning--caution"><strong>DRAFT—DO NOT DISTRIBUTE FOR USE.</strong> A clinician or certified bleeding-control instructor must review the words and diagram before this handout moves beyond draft.</div>

## Recognize and act

<p>Suspect life-threatening bleeding when blood flows continuously or spurts, pools on the ground, soaks clothing or bandages, or when the person shows shock or confusion. Act immediately.</p>

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Make the scene safe.</strong> Do not enter traffic, fire, violence, electrical, structural, or other danger. Use disposable gloves and eye protection when available; do not delay lifesaving pressure if barriers are unavailable.</li>
<li><strong>Activate help.</strong> Call emergency services, or point to a person and tell them to call and bring a bleeding-control kit. Put the phone on speaker if you are alone.</li>
<li><strong>Find and expose the injury.</strong> Remove or cut clothing only as needed to see where blood is coming from. Check for more than one wound while protecting the person from cold.</li>
<li><strong>Press continuously.</strong> Place gauze or a clean dressing directly on the bleeding site. Push hard with both hands and hold steady until bleeding stops or responders take over. Do not lift the dressing to check; add material on top if needed.</li>
</ol>

## If direct pressure does not control it

### Pack an appropriate wound—only if trained

<p>For a deep wound where a tourniquet cannot be used, such as the groin, armpit, shoulder, or neck, tightly pack bleeding-control gauze into the wound to the bleeding source, then press hard and continuously. Use hemostatic gauze if your training and product directions support it; otherwise use gauze from the bleeding-control kit. <strong>Do not pack the chest, abdomen, or an eye.</strong> Do not remove packing.</p>

### Use a commercial tourniquet for severe arm or leg bleeding

<ol class="procedure">
<li>Place a recognized <strong>commercial tourniquet</strong> 2–3 inches (5–8 cm) above the bleeding wound, between the wound and torso. Do not place it over the wound or a joint. If the wound cannot be located quickly, place it high and tight on the limb.</li>
<li>Pull the strap tight and fasten it. Twist the windlass until bleeding stops, then secure the windlass. It will hurt. If bleeding continues, tighten further or apply a second commercial tourniquet above the first if available and you are trained.</li>
<li>Write the application time below and on the tourniquet if it has a time field. Keep it uncovered and visible. Stay with the person, keep them warm, and monitor breathing until responders arrive.</li>
</ol>

<div class="warning warning--danger"><strong>Never</strong> remove an embedded object or probe a wound. Apply pressure around an embedded object. <strong>Never</strong> loosen, remove, or conceal an applied tourniquet; only emergency or medical professionals should remove it.</div>

<p><strong>Equipment boundary:</strong> This handout recommends a recognized commercial tourniquet and purpose-made bleeding-control gauze. It does not teach or recommend improvised tourniquets or improvised wound-packing material.</p>
