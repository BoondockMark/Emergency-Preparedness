<p class="lede">Plan before moving water containers, go-bags, sandbags, animal supplies, batteries, and other preparedness equipment. A lighter trip is usually the safer trip.</p>

<div class="warning warning--danger"><strong>STOP—do not lift or continue</strong> if the load shifts or is unstable; the route is wet, slippery, dark, steep, or obstructed; your grip is poor; or you feel pain, dizziness, shortness of breath, unusual weakness, or fatigue. Stop if the weight is beyond your capability. Set it down safely if you can—never try to “push through.”</div>

## Before you lift

<ol class="procedure">
<li><strong>Test without committing:</strong> read a weight label if present, then push, tilt, or lift one edge a few centimeters. If it is hard to control, do not lift it.</li>
<li><strong>Make it smaller or lighter:</strong> remove items; use smaller water containers; half-fill when appropriate; or divide food, animal feed, litter, and sandbags into more trips. Close lids and secure loose contents.</li>
<li><strong>Clear the whole route:</strong> choose the shortest usable path. Remove cords, rugs, debris, and low obstacles. Open doors, turn on lights, secure pets, and identify a level set-down spot before starting.</li>
<li><strong>Choose an aid:</strong> use a cart, hand truck, wagon, rolling bag, shoulder-height shelf, ramp, or other rated equipment when available. Keep loads low and secured; stay within the aid's rating. Do not ride on material-handling equipment.</li>
</ol>

## Lift and carry

  <figcaption id="lift-caption"><strong>Test → set → hold close → step.</strong> The safest posture cannot make an over-heavy or unstable load safe. <span class="credit">Original Fire Watch diagram; technical basis: OSHA and NIOSH.</span></figcaption>
</figure>

<ol class="procedure">
<li>Wear secure, closed-toe footwear. Stand on firm, dry, stable footing with feet apart and one foot slightly forward. Get close to the load.</li>
<li>Use secure handles or opposite lower corners. Keep a neutral, comfortable back position; bend at the hips and knees as your abilities allow. Keep fingers away from pinch points.</li>
<li>Brace gently and rise smoothly with the load close to your body. Do not jerk. Keep the load between knee and shoulder height when practical, and keep your view clear.</li>
<li><strong>Never twist while holding the load.</strong> Turn your whole body by taking small steps. Set down smoothly on a stable surface; protect fingers and toes.</li>
</ol>

<div class="callout"><strong>No universal “safe weight” applies to everyone.</strong> Size, shape, distance from the body, repetition, terrain, health, and individual capability all matter. When unsure, divide the load, use an aid, or ask for help.</div>

<!-- pagebreak -->

<p class="lede">Plan shared carries and accessible alternatives before an evacuation, outage, or storm makes the job urgent.</p>

## Two-person carry

<ol class="procedure">
<li>Do not use a team carry for a load that is unstable, leaking, hard to grip, or beyond the pair's combined control. Divide it or use a rated aid instead.</li>
<li>Agree on the destination, route, set-down point, and commands. The person with the clearest view is the <strong>caller</strong>. Match grip and carrying height.</li>
<li>The caller asks, “Ready?” Both confirm. On “Lift,” rise together. Walk with short, matched steps; do not twist. Either person may call “Stop.”</li>
<li>On “Down,” both lower together. If a grip slips, the load shifts, the route changes, or either person has pain or fatigue, call “Stop” immediately—do not try to save a falling load.</li>
</ol>

## Alternatives and help arranged in advance

<ul class="checklist">
<li>Store frequently used supplies within easy reach and near where they will be used. Avoid floor-level and overhead storage when possible.</li>
<li>Choose smaller containers, rolling go-bags, carts with brakes, long-handled tools, delivery, or staged caches. Transfer supplies while seated only if the seat is stable and the motion is comfortable.</li>
<li>Do not hang heavy bags on a wheelchair, walker, or mobility device unless its manufacturer permits it; added weight can affect stability and braking. Keep mobility-device paths wide and clear.</li>
<li>Ask a household member, neighbor, caregiver, animal-assistance contact, or community group now. Practice once in ordinary conditions and confirm who brings the cart, who handles animals, and who is the backup.</li>
</ul>

## Supplies are not injured people

<div class="warning warning--danger"><strong>These lifting steps are for supplies—not for moving an injured person.</strong> Moving someone can worsen spine or other injuries. Call emergency services, follow dispatcher instructions, and do not move the person unless there is an immediate danger (such as fire, smoke, traffic, or collapse) or the dispatcher directs you to move them.</div>
