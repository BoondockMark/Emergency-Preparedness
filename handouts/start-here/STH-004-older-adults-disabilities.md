<p class="lede">Plan around what you need to stay safe, communicate, move, receive support, and use essential equipment—not around a label or diagnosis.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>If danger is immediate:</strong> follow official instructions and move to safety. Tell responders about urgent access, communication, medical, or equipment needs.</div>

## Start with your usual day

Think through each activity you may need to do at home, while leaving, in transit, and at a shelter. Include supports you use sometimes as well as every day.

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>I identified how I receive warnings and communicate if power, internet, speech, hearing, or vision access changes.</li>
<li>I planned how I will move through doorways, stairs, smoke, debris, or unfamiliar spaces.</li>
<li>I identified any help I want with transfers, positioning, personal care, eating, or drinking.</li>
<li>I packed sensory supports and wrote down ways to reduce noise, light, touch, or crowding.</li>
<li>I chose cognitive supports, such as a simple sequence, pictures, reminders, familiar items, or a calm guide.</li>
</ul></div>

## Transportation and shelter prompts

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Ask local emergency management and transportation providers how to request accessible evacuation transportation. What is the backup?</li>
<li>Will the ride accommodate the person, mobility device, support person, service animal, and essential supplies together?</li>
<li>Which destinations can meet access needs? Confirm entrances, routes, toilets, cots, charging, refrigeration, communication access, and personal assistance.</li>
<li>How will the household reconnect if separated? Keep destination and contact information in more than one accessible format.</li>
<li>Practice the route and boarding plan without creating risk. Update the plan when needs, equipment, or providers change.</li>
</ul></div>

<div class="warning warning--caution"><strong>Do not assume a shelter has the support or equipment you need.</strong> Ask the responsible local authority before an emergency, and keep an alternate destination and transportation plan.</div>

## Build a support network

Choose people you trust—near home, work, or other regular locations. A network member should agree before being included.

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Share only the parts of the plan each person needs. Exchange keys or access instructions only if you choose.</li>
<li>Agree who will check in, who can assist, and who has a backup role.</li>
<li>Show helpers how you prefer to communicate and how to assist with mobility or transfers; do not let anyone guess or improvise unsafe techniques.</li>
<li>Practice contacting one another and review the plan at least when information or needs change.</li>
</ul></div>

## Plan for health, equipment, and power

<div class="warning"><strong>Make these plans with the right experts.</strong> Discuss medical contingencies with relevant clinicians; equipment operation and backup power with the supplier or manufacturer; outage programs with the utility; and evacuation, shelter, and emergency-power options with local authorities. This handout does not establish safe device runtimes, battery sizes, medication changes, or clinical instructions.</div>

## Medications and refrigeration

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Keep a current list of medication names, doses, schedules, allergies, prescriber, and pharmacy.</li>
<li>Ask the prescriber and pharmacist what to do if a dose, refill, evacuation, contamination, or temperature control is disrupted.</li>
<li>For each refrigerated item, get product-specific storage and outage instructions from the pharmacist, clinician, or manufacturer.</li>
<li>Use a cooler or discard a product only according to verified product-specific instructions. Do not guess from appearance or a general time limit.</li>
<li>Plan how medicines and supplies will travel with you, within required temperature limits and legal requirements.</li>
</ul></div>

## Durable medical equipment and electricity

Include mobility, breathing, communication, monitoring, transfer, feeding, and other equipment you rely on.

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Record the exact equipment name, model, serial number, supplier, and support number.</li>
<li>Ask the supplier or manufacturer for written shutdown, transport, storage, charging, and backup instructions for the exact model.</li>
<li>Ask the clinician for a written contingency plan if equipment is unavailable, damaged, or loses power.</li>
<li>Identify compatible backup equipment and power sources. Have a qualified provider confirm connections, ventilation, capacity, maintenance, and safe location.</li>
<li>Register for any applicable utility or local emergency program, but do not treat registration as a guarantee of priority restoration or evacuation help.</li>
<li>Test and maintain batteries or backup systems only as directed. Never use a fuel-burning generator indoors, in a garage, or near openings.</li>
</ul></div>

## Service animals

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Plan for the animal to remain with its handler during transportation and sheltering; ask local authorities about accessible arrangements.</li>
<li>Pack food, water, medicines, bowls, waste supplies, leash or harness, identification, and veterinary information.</li>
<li>Identify alternate handlers and care options if the handler cannot provide care.</li>
<li>Do not separate, distract, feed, or handle a service animal without the handler's permission.</li>
</ul></div>

## Protect this information

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Include only information you want another person to see; leave any field blank.</li>
<li>Keep paper copies in a closed, water-resistant holder. Do not display sensitive details on the outside of a bag.</li>
<li>Protect digital copies with device access controls and secure sharing. Avoid public links or unencrypted messages for sensitive records.</li>
<li>Give copies only to people you choose. Retrieve old copies and update or destroy them securely when information changes.</li>
<li>Carry essential instructions, but consider keeping detailed records separately with a trusted contact or in protected storage.</li>
</ul></div>

## Essential instructions to copy and carry

Written instructions should be specific to you and the exact product or support. Ask the appropriate clinician, pharmacist, supplier, or manufacturer to verify them.

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Current medication list, allergies, prescriber, pharmacy, and verified missed-dose or disruption instructions</li>
<li>Care, communication, behavior-support, mobility, transfer, positioning, or personal-assistance instructions you choose to share</li>
<li>Equipment model information and verified setup, shutdown, transport, cleaning, charging, and backup instructions</li>
<li>Verified refrigeration requirements and what to do after a temperature excursion</li>
<li>Emergency contacts, support-network roles, insurance details, and copies of identification you choose to carry</li>
<li>Advance directives or other legal documents, if applicable, stored and shared according to your wishes and local law</li>
</ul></div>

## Before you call the plan ready

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>I can reach alerts, plans, and contacts in the formats I use.</li>
<li>My primary and backup transportation plans address my access needs.</li>
<li>My likely destinations can support me, my equipment, and my service animal.</li>
<li>My network knows its roles and has practiced the plan.</li>
<li>My medication, refrigeration, equipment, and power contingencies were verified by the relevant experts.</li>
<li>My go-bag contains the supplies and copies of instructions I chose to carry.</li>
<li>My portable worksheet is current, protected, and shared only as I choose.</li>
</ul></div>
