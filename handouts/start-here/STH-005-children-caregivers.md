<p class="lede">Plan with children, caregivers, schools, and childcare programs so every child has a safe handoff and a familiar reunion plan.</p>

<div class="warning"><strong>Keep adult responsibilities with adults:</strong> children may practice simple, age-appropriate actions, but should not enter danger, retrieve supplies, care for other children, operate emergency equipment, or delay leaving to complete a task.</div>

## Plan with—not for—children

<div class="grid">
<div>

### Explain and practice calmly

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Describe emergencies likely where you live in simple, honest words: what may happen, what adults will do, and how the child can stay safe.</li>
<li>Teach one or two actions at a time. Younger children can state their full name and follow a familiar adult; older children can practice contacting the family's out-of-area contact.</li>
<li>Show the official alert sounds and messages your household may receive. Explain which trusted adults and local authorities give instructions.</li>
<li>Practice without surprise or frightening detail. Ask the child to explain the plan back in their own words.</li>
</ul></div>

### Make identification usable

Place a contact card in each child's bag or clothing pocket; do not display private information on the outside. Include:

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>child's name and preferred language or communication method;</li>
<li>primary and backup contacts;</li>
<li>approved pickup people;</li>
<li>essential medical, allergy, medication, feeding, access, and calming information; and</li>
<li>school or childcare name and phone number.</li>
</ul></div>

</div>
<div>

### Coordinate every care setting

Ask the school, childcare program, after-school program, and each caregiver:

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>How will you notify families, and what is the backup method?</li>
<li>Where will children shelter, evacuate, and reunify?</li>
<li>What identification is required at pickup?</li>
<li>How do I update authorized pickup contacts?</li>
<li>How are custody orders and medical or access needs handled?</li>
<li>How are medication, feeding supplies, breast milk or formula, and refrigeration or backup power handled?</li>
</ul></div>

Give each care setting current contact, custody, consent, and medical documents in the form it requires. Keep protected copies with the household plan. Tell the program promptly when anything changes.

### Choose reunion arrangements

Select a near-home meeting place, a place outside the neighborhood, and an out-of-area contact. Decide who may pick up each child and who is the backup. Children should remain with the responsible adult or program until an approved pickup person completes its release process.

</div>
</div>

## Pack for infants and young children

<div class="checklist"><ul>
<li>Age-appropriate food, ready-to-feed formula if used, feeding supplies, and safe water consistent with current caregiver instructions</li>
<li>Diapers or training supplies, wipes, disposal bags, hygiene items, and changes of clothing</li>
<li>Required medications and copies of current instructions or authorizations</li>
<li>Safe carrier, stroller, or other mobility support, plus weather protection</li>
<li>Contact card, recent photo, copies of essential records, and a small familiar comfort item</li>
</ul></div>

Keep supplies within expiration dates and suitable for the child's current size and needs. See **STH-002 — Preparedness Kit Checklist** for shared household supplies.

## After an emergency

Offer calm, truthful reassurance and restore familiar routines when possible. Listen without forcing a child to talk, limit repeated exposure to upsetting coverage, and expect needs to differ by age and experience. If distress persists, worsens, or interferes with daily life, contact an appropriate qualified health, mental-health, school, or childcare professional for support. This handout does not provide medical advice.
